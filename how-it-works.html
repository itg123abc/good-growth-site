How it works

A cartridge, a reusable reactor, and a local point of production.

The Good Growth system is designed to keep local production practical: begin with a compact cartridge, grow it in a reusable reactor, then apply the output through methods that fit real farm operations.

1. Start with a compact cartridge

Compact starter cartridges provide the inputs for each production cycle, including the mid-cycle growth inputs. The reusable reactor supports recurring production.

2. Grow in a reusable reactor

The process uses a reusable reactor and mid-cycle growth inputs near a grower or local partner. Local production does not have to mean every grower owns or operates a reactor.

3. Plan application around the site

The design goal is integration with the irrigation equipment growers already use. Pilots will help us understand what connects well and how to improve the fit, while also exploring compatible spraying methods.

Picture the working routine

What would running a Good Growth reactor involve?

The design aims to make recurring local production practical. For an operator, the work centers on preparing a run, checking progress, adding the included mid-cycle inputs, and preparing for application and the next cycle.

Prepare the run

Set up the reactor and starter inputs with a production schedule linked to the intended application.

Check and support

Monitor the run and add the included growth inputs mid-cycle. The equipment does not remove the need for operator checks.

Apply and reset

Plan handling and delivery through suitable equipment, then clean and prepare the system for another cycle.

How much time and attention should I expect?

The current planning baseline is approximately two weeks per production cycle. That is elapsed production time, not continuous hands-on work. Under normal operating conditions, we expect routine checks and tasks to take around 5–10 minutes a day, with additional time for setup and cleaning. This is an estimate we’ll check during pilots.

Pilot planning would identify the operator, instructions, equipment needs, and support. The goal is practical, repeatable work—not a claim of unattended or maintenance-free production.

How would Good Growth fit my irrigation or spraying equipment?

The design goal is adaptability to the irrigation systems growers already use. Pilots will help establish what connects well and what needs adjustment, including filtration, delivery, and compatible spraying methods. The application method and useful handling window need to be established before use.

What if I prefer to buy from a local provider?

A nearby retailer, co-op, or other agricultural partner could operate the reactor. You would access the output without managing production equipment yourself. Local partner availability and operating arrangements are still part of the development work.
