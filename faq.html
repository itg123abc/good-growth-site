Practical questions

Frequently asked questions.

Explore the system, the local production model, and what a pilot conversation could cover. We are developing the platform with practical use in mind; pilot arrangements are worked out together before participation.

Have another question? Let's talk →
What is Good Growth building?

We’re building a system for producing agricultural microbial inputs at a farm or through a nearby agricultural partner. It uses a reusable reactor and starter cartridges, which come with the additional growth inputs needed partway through each cycle. You keep using the reactor and start each new cycle with a fresh cartridge. The idea is to bring production closer to where the inputs are used—and make it something that fits into the work and budget of a real operation.

How can I get involved?

Tell us a little about yourself, what you grow or do, and what caught your interest. You might want to try the inputs, produce at your farm, purchase from a future local provider, or help with testing. We’d like to hear from potential production partners too. Good Growth is still in development, so you don’t need to arrive with a proposal or know exactly where you fit. A question or an idea is enough to start.

Do I need my own reactor?

No. Some growers may want to produce at their own farm, while others would rather buy what they need from a nearby retailer, co-op, or local producer. We’re developing the system with both options in mind. If you prefer purchasing locally, you wouldn’t be responsible for running the production equipment. We’re still identifying potential providers, so telling us where you’re located helps us understand where there’s interest.

What would I need to do to run the Good Growth reactor?

If you’re producing at your farm, you or someone on your team would look after the system. Under normal operating conditions, we expect routine checks and tasks to take around 5–10 minutes a day, with more time needed for setup and cleaning between cycles. That’s our current estimate, which we’ll check during pilots. The work includes checking the run and adding the included growth inputs partway through. We’d walk through your setup together first, including who would operate it and what support they might need.

Can I use Good Growth with my existing irrigation or spraying equipment?

That’s what we’re designing it for. Our goal is to make the system adaptable to any irrigation setup, and we want to learn how it fits the equipment growers already have. Part of testing will be finding out what connects easily and what needs improving. We’re looking at spraying methods too. We’d go over your equipment, filters, and application method with you to check the fit before using it.

How long does a production cycle take?

We’re currently planning around two weeks per cycle, with testing ahead to narrow down the best production endpoint and application timing. Early reactor observations suggest there may be some flexibility in when the output can be used, which we’re working to confirm through biological measurements. The goal is to give growers room to plan applications around their operation while maintaining the quality of what they apply.

Which crops and operations can participate?

We’re open to hearing from growers across all crops and types of operations. Part of this work is finding out where the system can be most helpful and which applications are worth testing first. Tell us what you grow, where you’re located, and how you’d like to use the inputs through our contact form.

What would taking part in a pilot look like?

It starts with a discussion about your operation and what you’d like to try. That might be comparing the inputs with your usual practice or finding out what running a Good Growth reactor at your farm would involve. Together, we’d work out what to test, what to record, when to do it, and who handles each task. We’d also discuss costs before you commit. Your feedback on anything that adds work, causes confusion, or could be simpler would help us improve the system.

What would a pilot cost?

The cost will depend on whether you’re trying the inputs at your site or taking part in on-site production. We’d talk through the equipment, inputs, testing, and support involved, then outline the costs and responsibilities before you decide to participate.

What are you hoping to learn from the pilots?

We want to find out whether the system makes sense on a real operation. That includes how the inputs perform, how they move through application equipment, how much work production takes, and what it costs. A trial might also look at plant establishment or root growth, with a comparison agreed beforehand. We haven’t established those benefits yet. The results—and your experience using the Good Growth reactor—will help us decide what works, what needs changing, and what to test next.

How will you check the quality of what’s produced?

We need to check which microbes are present, how many are alive, whether there’s contamination, and how consistent the output is from one batch to the next. We also need to understand what happens during handling and application. Those checks are part of the testing we’re developing, including the standards a batch would need to meet. Appearance or visible activity alone can’t tell us whether the output is suitable to use.
